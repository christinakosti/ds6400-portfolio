# Advanced Machine Learning I - DS 6400 Portfolio

This repository contains my coursework and computational explorations from **DS 6400: Advanced Machine Learning I** at the University of Virginia.

The portfolio covers statistical learning methods, model evaluation, regularization, and model interpretation through mathematical explanations, reproducible R code, and visualizations.

Where relevant, I incorporate examples from **exercise physiology, biomedical research, and wearable technology** to connect statistical methods with research questions in my field.

📖 **[Explore the Interactive Portfolio](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/)**

## Featured applied examples

| Application | Where to look | What the analysis demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| **Wearables → next-day performance** | **Chapter 4** · [Case study](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/04-random-forests.html#case-study-predicting-next-day-performance) · [Sleep × training-strain interaction](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/04-random-forests.html#do-sleep-and-training-strain-interact) | A **simulated longitudinal wearable dataset** with 160 individuals and 2,240 person-days. Random Forest predicts next-day performance from sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, training strain, and other features. The analysis examines out-of-bag error, permutation importance, nonlinearities, and interactions. |
| **Muscle strength → nonlinear modeling** | **Chapter 5** · [Muscle-strength example](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/05-polynomial-regression.html#applied-research-example-muscle-strength-assessment) · [Polynomial basis comparison](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/05-polynomial-regression.html#comparison-of-polynomial-bases) | Compares **natural/raw, Bernstein, and orthogonal polynomial bases** using simulated age–strength data. The chapter also connects polynomial modeling to my published research involving knee-joint angles and isokinetic strength assessment. |
| **Electrical impedance myography → handgrip strength** | **Chapter 7** · [Physiological example](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/07-regularization-elastic-net.html#computational-exploration-a-physiological-example) · [Feature selection](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/07-regularization-elastic-net.html#variable-selection-and-the-grouping-effect) | Compares **Ridge, LASSO, and Elastic Net** for predicting handgrip strength using **243 correlated, simulated EIM features**. Includes coefficient paths, cross-validation, correlated-feature selection, and independent test-set evaluation. |
| **Physical activity, sedentary behavior → HDL cholesterol** | **Chapter 8** · [Information-sharing models](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/HW9.html#comparing-information-sharing-models) · [Nonlinear response surfaces](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/HW9.html#group-specific-nonlinear-response-surfaces) · [Contrast coding](https://christinakosti.github.io/ds6400-portfolio/HW9.html#sensitivity-to-contrast-coding) | Uses **NHANES data** to examine how age and BMI relate to HDL cholesterol across physical-activity and television-viewing groups. Compares shared and group-specific regression models, nonlinear spline relationships, selective regularization, and sensitivity to contrast coding. |

**Data transparency:** The wearable-performance, age–strength, and EIM datasets are **simulated teaching examples**, not clinical validation studies. Chapter 8 uses observational NHANES survey data. The examples illustrate statistical methods and should not be interpreted as evidence of causal relationships.
